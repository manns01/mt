About Me

I am a PhD researcher at the Indian Institute of Science (IISc), Bangalore, working at the intersection of cosmology, gravitational lensing and dark matter. My research interests include gravitational microlensing, wave-optics lensing, astroparticle physics and the use of computational and machine-learning methods in cosmology.

I have previously worked as a visiting researcher at the Max Planck Institute for Gravitational Physics (Albert Einstein Institute) in Potsdam, Germany. I hold an M.S. in Physics from IISc and a B.Sc. (Hons.) in Physics from St. Stephen's College, University of Delhi.

More broadly, I am interested in understanding dark matter and the Universe through gravitational lensing, numerical simulations and data-driven methods. Outside academia, I enjoy travelling and photography.

Research areas

Gravitational Lensing — strong, weak and microlensing, with an emphasis on wave-optics effects and non-standard lensing regimes.

Dark Matter — compact dark matter, primordial black holes and ultralight/wave dark matter.

Wave Optics — gravitational lensing when wave effects become important across electromagnetic and gravitational-wave frequencies.

Computational Cosmology — numerical simulations, scientific computing and machine-learning methods for cosmological inference.
